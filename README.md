# 🦛 Hippo Baked Snacks — Relaunch Strategy
### *A Brand Manager's Playbook | By G. Manideep*

---

## About This Project

This is a self-initiated marketing case study where I took on the role of **Brand Manager / CMO** for Hippo Baked Snacks — a brand that succeeded in marketing but failed in execution. The goal was to identify why it failed and build a complete relaunch strategy that fixes the root problems, not just the surface ones.

All visuals were concept-directed by me and generated using AI as a design tool. The strategy, insights, campaign ideas, and creative decisions are my own.

---

## 1. Brand Background — Who Was Hippo?

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Brand** | Hippo Baked Snacks |
| **Company** | Parle Agro |
| **Launch Year** | 2009 |
| **Category** | FMCG / Snacks |
| **Product Type** | Baked wheat & potato snack |
| **Competitors** | Lays, Kurkure, Bingo |
| **Target Audience** | Teenagers, college students, young adults |
| **USP** | "Light, baked, healthier than fried chips" |

Hippo launched in 2009 with one of India's most memorable snack brand identities — a bold black hippo silhouette mascot, bright packaging, and a quirky personality that stood out on shelves. It became famous for an early digital campaign where customers tweeted when Hippo was out of stock, and the brand responded in real time. One of India's first successful social media engagement campaigns.

**And then it disappeared.**

---

## 2. Why Hippo Failed — The Real Diagnosis

Most people say Hippo failed because of competition. That's the surface answer.

The real answer is simpler and more fixable:

> **Hippo built demand it couldn't fulfill. That's worse than never building demand at all.**

| What Went Wrong | Why It Mattered |
|---|---|
| Irregular supply to retailers | Customers couldn't find the product |
| Low retailer margins | Shopkeepers preferred stocking Lays and Bingo |
| No distribution depth | Only present in select cities/stores |
| Strong competitor shelf dominance | No space left for Hippo even when supply existed |

Hippo's marketing was ahead of its time. Its operations were not.

---

## 3. The Strategic Insight

> *"Don't fight for shelf space you can't win. Create a channel where shelves don't exist."*

Instead of trying to out-distribute PepsiCo and ITC with the same traditional retail model — **flip the model entirely.**

**Primary distribution: Quick Commerce**
Zepto · Blinkit · BigBasket

This solves every original problem:
- No shelf space needed
- No retailer margin negotiations
- Consistent availability guaranteed
- Delivery in under 15 minutes

**Secondary distribution:** Traditional kirana and modern trade wherever quick commerce doesn't reach — but this is no longer the primary battlefield.

## 4. Campaign Strategy

### 4.1 Campaign Line

> **"Not in stores. At your door. Still Hippo."**

This line does three things simultaneously:
- Acknowledges the original failure honestly
- Positions quick commerce as a feature, not a limitation
- Defends brand identity — nothing changed, we just moved

### 4.2 Campaign Rollout Phases

| Phase | Activity | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | Full campaign across OOH, digital, quick commerce listings | Week 1 |
| Influencer Wave | PR kits sent, content goes live | Week 2 |
| Organic Buzz | Caricature content starts appearing on social media | Week 2 onwards |
| Expansion | New cities, new flavors drop | Month 3 |

The Hippo mascot — bold black silhouette — walks along the road wearing the Hippo snack packet as a backpack.

**Copy:** *"Not in stores. At your door. Still Hippo."*

## 6. Product Strategy

### 6.1 Keep What Worked
- Baked positioning (more relevant now than ever — health consciousness is mainstream)
- Original flavors: Indian Masala, Chinese Manchurian, Tomato, Cheese, Yoghurt Mint
- Bold black silhouette mascot — it's brand equity, don't touch it

### 6.2 Add Two New Flavours
- **BBQ Smoke** — high social media traction flavor profile
- **Hippo Hot Chilli** — addresses the spicy snack segment Hippo never owned

### 6.3 Packaging
Same original design language — solid color per flavor, bold black hippo silhouette, heavy white typography. Each pack includes a **QR code** on the back.

## 7. The QR Experience — "Scan. Strike a Pose. Transform Yourself."

Every Hippo pack has a QR code. Scanning it opens a **caricature camera.**

**How it works:**
1. Scan QR on pack
2. Camera opens
3. Take a selfie
4. AI converts it into a funny exaggerated caricature — oversized head, bold ink style, amusement park artist style
5. Download or share directly to Instagram / Snapchat

**Why no Hippo branding on the caricature:**
If it has a logo, it's an ad. People don't share ads.
If it's just a funny version of themselves, they share it everywhere.
The QR on the pack already did the brand's job. The caricature just needs to be shareable.

![QR Camera Experience](./visuals/qr_caricature.png)

## 8. Hippo Flight — The Sampler Box

**Concept borrowed from:** Beer flights — you don't pick one, you try all.

A sampler box containing **7 small individual pouches** — one of each flavor including the 2 new ones.

**Box design:**
- Yellow dominant with bold red top strip
- Black hippo silhouette split across the box — ears above the flavor window, mouth below
- Center window shows 7 flavor columns, each with solid color background and ingredient illustration
- Copy: **"Can't Pick One? Don't."**
- QR on each pouch gives a **discount on next full-size purchase** — built-in retention mechanic

**Why this works:**
- Lowers entry barrier for new customers (try before committing to one flavor)
- The discount QR pulls them back for a second purchase
- One product that does acquisition and retention simultaneously

![Hippo Flight Box](./visuals/hippo_flight.png)

## 9. Influencer PR Kit — "Something's Coming"

Sent to micro-influencers (50K–500K followers) in food, college life, and nostalgia content niches. **No product inside.**

**Box contents:**
| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Fun caricature sticker of the influencer | Personal, shareable, they'll post it |
| Hippo mascot keychain | Brand recall object |
| Launch date card | Creates anticipation |
| 5 black cards with flavor names | Mystery and intrigue |
| 2 red scratch cards | New flavors hidden under scratch panel |

**Why no product in the PR kit:**
Product creates a review. Mystery creates content.
An influencer posting "I got this weird box with my own caricature in it and scratch cards" generates more organic curiosity than "here's a snack review."

![Influencer PR Kit](./visuals/pr_kit.png)

## 10. Social Media Strategy — Let the Caricature Do the Work

No branded hashtag campaigns. No forced engagement mechanics.

The social media strategy is entirely driven by one thing — **the caricature.**

**How it works organically:**
1. Someone orders Hippo on Zepto or Blinkit
2. They scan the QR on the pack
3. They get a funny caricature of themselves
4. They post it on Instagram, Snapchat, or WhatsApp because it's funny and personal — not because Hippo asked them to

**Why this works:**
- People share things that are about themselves, not about brands
- A caricature is inherently shareable — it's funny, it's personal, it's visual
- Every post organically shows the Hippo pack in context — real purchase, real person
- WhatsApp sharing means it reaches people outside social media algorithms — friends, family groups, college chats

**The brand never has to say "share this."**
The product earns the share by being worth sharing.

## 11. Distribution Model

| Channel | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Zepto / Blinkit / BigBasket | Primary | No shelf politics, guaranteed availability, Gen Z native |
| Kirana stores | Secondary | Where quick commerce doesn't reach |
| Modern trade (DMart, Reliance) | Secondary | Brand visibility, Hippo Flight placement |

**Year 1 rule:** Own 5 cities deeply before going national. Don't repeat the original mistake of spreading thin.

---

## 12. Key Marketing Lessons This Strategy Is Built On

> **Lesson 1:** A great product alone is not enough.

> **Lesson 2:** Distribution IS marketing in FMCG.

> **Lesson 3:** Build your supply before you build your demand.

> **Lesson 4:** Retailer relationships can make or break a brand — or you can bypass retailers entirely.

> **Lesson 5:** The best viral marketing doesn't look like marketing.
